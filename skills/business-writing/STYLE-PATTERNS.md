# Business Writing Style Patterns

This file captures reusable style preferences learned from repeated B2B business communication. It is not a customer-specific style profile.

## 1. Overall tone
- Professional, natural, concise.
- Friendly without sounding like customer service.
- Firm when a commercial boundary is real.
- Warm enough to preserve the relationship, especially with existing customers.
- Avoid ceremonial corporate language unless the situation genuinely requires it.

## 2. Structure
- Lead with the actual business point.
- Keep one message focused on the current decision or update.
- Use short paragraphs; bullets only when they improve clarity.
- Put required confirmation/action where the customer can see it easily.
- Do not add a long summary after the key point has already been made.

## 3. What to avoid
- Excessive apology.
- Repeated `thank you very much` or overly enthusiastic gratitude.
- Robotic phrases such as `we are pleased to inform you` for routine updates.
- Weak wording that makes a hard constraint sound negotiable when it is not.
- Long explanations of internal production details that do not help the customer's decision.
- Generic closings that add no value.
- Mechanical translation of Chinese sentence order into English.

## 4. Existing-customer behavior
- Reduce ceremony as the relationship matures.
- Do not repeat company introduction or full background unnecessarily.
- Routine updates can be direct: status → attachment/reference → action needed.
- Preserve a cooperative tone without sounding over-familiar.

## 5. Negotiation behavior
- Explain the commercial or operational basis before defending a position.
- Do not sound apologetic for a structural MOQ, cost, or technical constraint.
- Do not hide the limitation; state it clearly after giving the reason.
- If there is no approved concession, keep the door open without implying one.

## 6. Follow-up behavior
- Follow up for a reason, not merely because time passed.
- Use project context, a useful update, a decision point, or relationship value to re-enter the conversation.
- Avoid repeated generic chasing.
- For a high-value mature project, concern and continuity can be warmer, but never desperate.

## 7. Revision behavior
When the user asks to change one part of a draft:
- preserve the parts that were already acceptable,
- change the requested logic or tone first,
- only adjust surrounding sentences when needed for consistency,
- do not rewrite the entire message into a different style without reason.

## 8. Information discipline
- Preserve exact numbers, product relationships, pack structures, dates, and confirmed facts supplied by the user.
- Do not omit a fact merely to make the message shorter when that fact is commercially important.
- Do not add unsupported claims to make the email sound stronger.

## 9. Preferred default
If no stronger instruction applies, aim for: `clear + commercially aware + natural + concise`.
