# GC-PRO-013 — Credibility Building with Proof and NDA Boundary

## Scenario
A new prospect needs confidence that the supplier can handle a meaningful OEM/private-label project. The supplier has concrete operational advantages and some customer references that may be publicly shareable, while other customer relationships are protected by confidentiality obligations.

## Business tension
The supplier wants to sound credible and differentiated without making vague claims or disclosing confidential customer information.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Lead with concrete, verifiable operational proof: years of experience, production capability, in-house development/tooling, quality systems, relevant audits, or project execution capability.
2. Use named customer references only when they are authorized or already appropriate for external use.
3. If additional relationships are confidential, say that further major accounts cannot be disclosed due to confidentiality rather than implying anonymous prestige without context.
4. Do not inflate capability with unsupported superlatives.
5. Connect credibility proof to the prospect's actual project need rather than listing every company fact.

## Strategy
Specific proof + selective social proof + confidentiality discipline.

## Communication pattern
Brief company relevance → 2–3 concrete differentiators → authorized customer/channel proof when available → concise NDA boundary for undisclosed accounts → return to the prospect's product/project.

## Anti-patterns
- `We are a leading professional supplier` with no evidence.
- Naming customers that are not approved for disclosure.
- Hiding all strong proof behind vague wording when authorized proof exists.
- Turning the first reply into a long corporate profile.
- Using confidential relationships as implied endorsements without clarity.

## Transferable principle
Credibility is strongest when it is concrete and scoped: show what can be proven, disclose only what is authorized, and tie the proof back to the customer's project.
