# GC-NEG-011 — Price Negotiation Near the Commercial Floor

## Scenario
A long-running project is close to agreement, but the customer's target remains below the supplier's current quote. The supplier has already recalculated the cost carefully and believes the current offer is close to the lowest workable level.

## Business tension
The supplier wants to show genuine effort and preserve the project without creating the impression that every firm statement is only another round of bargaining.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Confirm whether the current quote is genuinely near the approved commercial floor.
2. Explain that the price was re-evaluated carefully rather than presenting the number as arbitrary.
3. Avoid exposing unnecessary internal cost details.
4. Recognize the customer's effort and the value of the project without becoming emotionally dependent on the order.
5. If further management review may still be possible, describe it as a review rather than a promised reduction.
6. Keep quantity basis, packaging basis, and order conditions explicit so the low price is not detached from the assumptions that support it.

## Strategy
Credible firmness + relationship preservation + conditional final review.

## Communication pattern
Acknowledge the customer's continued effort → state that the offer has already been recalculated carefully → clarify the commercial basis → explain that the remaining room is very limited → keep a narrow door open for final internal review if appropriate.

## Anti-patterns
- Saying `this is our absolute final price` repeatedly and then reducing again without explanation.
- Dumping detailed internal cost sheets into the negotiation.
- Sounding cold after a long collaborative project.
- Promising that management will approve a further reduction.
- Omitting the quantity/order assumptions that support the price.

## Transferable principle
Near the commercial floor, credibility matters as much as the number: show that the position is calculated, conditional, and genuinely constrained.
