# GC-RISK-012 — Production Before Deposit as a Controlled Exception

## Scenario
An existing customer asks the supplier to begin production before the agreed deposit is received because of a tight delivery target. The supplier normally requires deposit before production due to prior payment-risk experience, but management may approve a one-time exception.

## Business tension
The supplier wants to protect payment discipline while supporting a trusted relationship and urgent schedule.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Separate the standard payment rule from the requested exception.
2. Identify why the normal rule exists and whether the exception has explicit management approval.
3. Do not describe unapproved production as already arranged.
4. If approved, document the exception clearly together with the customer's promised payment date/condition.
5. Keep any delivery acceleration as a separate approved condition; do not imply that early production guarantees the requested delivery date.
6. Preserve the long-term relationship without normalizing the exception as a new standard term.

## Strategy
Controlled flexibility + explicit approval + traceable condition.

## Communication pattern
Confirm the special approval → distinguish it from the normal policy → state what will proceed now → restate the customer's agreed deposit timing → clarify any separately approved delivery target → keep the tone cooperative rather than punitive.

## Anti-patterns
- Starting production before approval and describing it afterward as policy.
- Turning one exception into an implied permanent payment term.
- Guaranteeing accelerated delivery merely because production starts early.
- Using previous payment problems to shame the current customer.
- Leaving the promised deposit date undocumented.

## Transferable principle
Commercial exceptions should increase flexibility without erasing the rule: approval, scope, and conditions must remain explicit.
