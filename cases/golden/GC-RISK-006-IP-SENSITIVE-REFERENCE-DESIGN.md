# GC-RISK-006 — IP-Sensitive Reference Design

## Scenario
A prospect asks for a product that closely copies an existing branded, character-based, or otherwise potentially protected design.

## Business tension
The supplier wants to preserve the inquiry while avoiding unnecessary IP exposure or appearing unwilling to help.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Identify that 1:1 replication may create IP or design-right risk.
2. Do not make an unsupported legal conclusion about infringement.
3. Avoid simply rejecting the opportunity.
4. Redirect toward existing non-infringing styles, original development, or design elements that meet the customer's functional and aesthetic goals without copying protected content.
5. Keep MOQ, tooling, and commercial feasibility separate from the IP issue.

## Strategy
Risk-aware redirection + preserve sales opportunity.

## Communication pattern
Acknowledge the desired style → explain that exact replication of third-party branded/IP elements may create risk → recommend alternative existing or custom-developed designs → invite the customer to select a preferred direction for further quotation/development.

## Anti-patterns
- Stating definitively that a design is illegal without evidence.
- Agreeing to 1:1 copy before rights are clarified.
- Ending the inquiry with a bare refusal.
- Mixing IP risk with unrelated commercial objections in a way that confuses the customer.

## Transferable principle
When reference designs create possible IP exposure, redirect the project toward original or safer alternatives while keeping the commercial conversation alive.
