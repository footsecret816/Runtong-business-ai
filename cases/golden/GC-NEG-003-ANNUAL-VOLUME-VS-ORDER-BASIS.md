# GC-NEG-003 — Annual Volume vs Per-Order Pricing Basis

## Scenario
A customer presents an attractive annual purchasing volume, but the supplier's quoted unit price was calculated on the assumption that each purchase order or shipment would reach a specific production or container basis.

## Business tension
Customer frames the opportunity by annual potential.

Supplier economics depend on per-order production quantity, packing mix, shipment utilization, or other execution conditions.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Separate annual potential from actual order-by-order execution.
2. Identify exactly which quantity basis supports the current quotation.
3. Explain the pricing condition without sounding like the earlier offer was misleading.
4. Preserve the customer's long-term volume value while clarifying that the quoted price applies only when each order meets the stated basis.
5. If smaller individual orders are possible, treat revised pricing as a separate calculation rather than silently extending the original price.

## Strategy
Clarify commercial basis + preserve long-term opportunity.

## Communication pattern
Acknowledge the annual plan → clarify the per-order pricing assumption → restate the condition clearly → keep room to calculate alternatives if needed.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating annual volume as automatically equivalent to one production order.
- Quietly changing the quoted price without explaining the basis.
- Sounding accusatory because the customer described annual rather than per-order volume.
- Overloading the customer with internal cost calculations.

## Transferable principle
Always distinguish commercial potential from the operational quantity basis that actually supports a quoted price.
