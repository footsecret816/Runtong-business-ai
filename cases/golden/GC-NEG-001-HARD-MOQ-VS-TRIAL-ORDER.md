# GC-NEG-001 — Hard MOQ vs Trial Order

## Scenario
A B2B customer wants a small trial order, but the supplier has a substantially higher hard production MOQ caused by production economics or material batch constraints.

## Business tension
Customer risk: inventory and market validation.

Supplier risk: production inefficiency or material/process minimums.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Recognize the customer's trial-order logic.
2. Verify whether the MOQ is genuinely hard or commercially flexible.
3. Explain the production basis rather than calling it merely a company policy.
4. Avoid exposing unnecessary internal cost detail.
5. Do not offer an exception that has not been approved.
6. Keep the relationship open and, if feasible, explore alternative configurations or future-volume paths.

## Strategy
Firm + cooperative + concise.

## Communication pattern
Acknowledge the customer's concern → explain the operational reason → state the realistic minimum → keep the discussion open.

## Anti-patterns
- Repeating `our MOQ is X` without explanation.
- Excessive apology.
- Inventing exact cost differences.
- Promising a lower trial quantity without approval.
- Making the customer feel the supplier is unwilling to cooperate.

## Transferable principle
When a commercial limit is structural rather than arbitrary, explain the structure clearly and preserve willingness to cooperate.
