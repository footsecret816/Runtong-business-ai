# GC-TECH-002 — Internal Data vs Formal Third-Party Report

## Scenario
A customer requests a formal third-party technical or stability report for registration or compliance purposes. The supplier currently has internal test data but not the requested formal report format.

## Business tension
Customer needs document credibility and possible regulatory usability.

Supplier has technical evidence, but the available document form may not satisfy the customer's final use.

## Recommended reasoning pattern
1. Distinguish between lack of testing and lack of the requested report format.
2. State what evidence currently exists without overstating its regulatory value.
3. Clarify the intended use of the requested report when that materially affects the solution.
4. If appropriate, propose a staged path: use current data for technical review, then arrange formal third-party testing after the product/formula/specification is locked.
5. Keep any claim about regulatory acceptance as `TO_CONFIRM` unless verified.

## Strategy
Honest limitation + preserve technical credibility + give a practical next path.

## Communication pattern
Confirm current evidence → explain document limitation → propose a concrete route to formalization → avoid weakening the project unnecessarily.

## Anti-patterns
- Saying `we have no test` when internal data exists.
- Implying internal data is automatically regulator-accepted.
- Inventing standards or report acceptance rules.
- Promising a third-party report before scope, cost, timing, or feasibility is confirmed.

## Transferable principle
When evidence exists but the required form is missing, frame the gap precisely and solve the document-form problem rather than understating the underlying capability.
