# Case Extraction Guide

Use this guide when converting real business conversations into reusable cases.

## Goal
Preserve the business lesson while removing customer identity and unnecessary confidential detail.

## Extraction flow
1. Identify the real business tension.
2. Separate confirmed facts from interpretation.
3. Remove names, contacts, PO numbers, account-specific IDs, and unnecessary dates.
4. Generalize confidential exact prices, payment records, and sensitive quantities unless the numeric relationship itself is essential to the lesson.
5. Remove supplier identities unless the supplier role is technically necessary.
6. Rewrite the scenario so it can apply to another product/company without losing the business logic.
7. Preserve the reasoning sequence, preferred strategy, anti-patterns, and transferable principle.
8. Verify that the case cannot be used as evidence about any current customer.

## Keep when materially useful
- customer type or relationship stage,
- project stage,
- type of commercial/technical constraint,
- relative quantity/price relationship,
- type of requested document/test,
- decision sequence,
- communication objective,
- what made the preferred response effective.

## Remove or generalize
- customer/company names,
- individual names,
- email/phone/address,
- PO/SKU/order IDs,
- confidential exact prices,
- exact payment history,
- unnecessary exact dates,
- private supplier names,
- identifying shipment details,
- details that do not change the lesson.

## Case quality test
A good abstract case should pass all three tests:
1. **Portable** — useful in another company/product context.
2. **Non-identifying** — does not reveal the original customer's identity or confidential history.
3. **Decision-preserving** — still teaches why a strong business manager would choose one strategy over another.

## Important rule
Do not sanitize so aggressively that the business tension disappears. Remove identity, not reasoning value.
